> **B2B guide** — for equipment manufacturers who buy couplings in volume. This is the process we run on every OEM inquiry, and the five-piece RFQ packet is what makes a project fast instead of a loop.

## What OEM Means for Quick Couplings

OEM (original equipment manufacturer) supply of hydraulic quick couplings means the coupling becomes part of *your* product — a tractor's hydraulic outlet, a skid steer's coupler bank, an implement's line set. The coupling carries your part number, your packaging, and your quality bar, so the relationship is different from a stock purchase: it's a specification project.

Two flavors of OEM work:

| Type | What it is | Typical trigger |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Branded standard** | Standard ISO 7241/16028/5675 couplings, your part number + packaging | You want a reliable, tested source with your identity |
| **Custom engineered** | Modified dimensions, materials, threads, markings or features | Your design needs something the catalog doesn't offer |

Most OEM projects start as branded standard and add custom elements over time — threads, special seals, dual markings, color-coded sleeves.

## The RFQ Five-Piece Packet

A complete OEM RFQ answers five questions. Projects stall when pieces are missing:

| # | Piece | What it tells us |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | **Drawing or part number** | The exact geometry you need — or the standard you're referencing |
| 2 | **Application + duty** | Size/flow/pressure (see the [sizing guide](/blog/how-to-size-hydraulic-quick-coupling/)), fluid, cycles |
| 3 | **Threads and connections** | Port thread standard (NPT/BSPP/JIC/ORFS) — see the [thread guide](/blog/npt-vs-bspp-vs-bspt-vs-jic-vs-orfs/) |
| 4 | **Material + finish** | Steel/stainless/brass, plating, color coding |
| 5 | **Annual volume + target price** | What production scale and cost structure we design for |

With the five pieces, a serious supplier can return a technical proposal with samples in the picture. Without them, every answer is a guess and the project loops.

## Drawing → Sample → Production

The standard sequence, with realistic gates:

1. **Technical review** — we check the drawing against the ISO standards, flag interchange conflicts, and confirm the threads/materials are available at your volume. **Gate:** mutual agreement on the spec.
2. **Sample run** — typically a small batch (e.g. 10–50 pieces) from production tooling, not prototypes. Samples confirm the drawing is manufacturable and give you parts to test-fit on your machine.
3. **Qualification** — you test-fit, we pressure-test and dimension-check against the drawing; both sides sign off the sample report. **Gate:** sample approval.
4. **Production run** — the approved spec goes to the line. Key: the production parts must match the approved samples, not the original drawing alone — drawings drift, samples don't.
5. **Ongoing QC** — batch inspection against the approved sample: dimensions, pressure test, seal integrity, plating thickness.

## The Qualification Checks

Before a batch is approved, the checks that catch the expensive failures:

| Check | What it catches |
|:--|:--|
| **Dimensional** (bore, groove position, thread fit) | Interchange failures, stuck sleeves |
| **Pressure test** (proof + burst sample) | Wrong material, thin wall, bad seal seat |
| **Leak test** at rated pressure | Poppet seal quality |
| **Cycle test** (connect/disconnect N cycles) | Sleeve spring, ball retention, wear |
| **Material/plating check** | Corrosion resistance, thread galling |

The OEM buyer should always ask: *what does the batch inspection report cover?* A supplier that can't show dimensional + pressure data on a batch is selling trust, not quality.

## Packaging and Marking

The details that make an OEM part "yours":

- **Your part number** laser-marked or stamped on the body (space permitting) — critical for traceability.
- **Standard/type marking** (e.g. ISO 7241-1-A) — helps your dealers identify the part later.
- **Your packaging** — boxes, barcode labels, per-set kits (nipple + coupler + dust caps).
- **Kitting** — many OEM couplings ship as matched pairs with caps, ready for the machine line.

Marking is often the *first* custom element an OEM adds, and it's the cheapest.

## FAQ

**Q: What's the minimum order for OEM couplings?**
A: It depends on the part — branded standard parts can start at modest volumes; custom-engineered parts need larger runs to amortize tooling. The RFQ packet's volume figure is what makes this answerable.

**Q: Can you match an existing coupling we buy elsewhere?**
A: Usually yes — with the drawing or a sample, we can match standard couplings and quote. That's the most common way OEM relationships start.

**Q: How long does the sample stage take?**
A: For standard couplings with minor customization, samples can move in weeks. Custom-engineered geometry extends it. The gates (spec → sample → qualify → produce) are where the real timeline is set.

**Q: Do we need our own drawing?**
A: No — if you have a part number or a competitor's sample, we can reverse-specify it against the ISO standard and return a drawing for your approval. That's part of the technical review.

**Q: What if our volume changes after the first run?**
A: Volume flexibility is normal — the pricing structure (tooling amortization, per-piece cost) is designed at RFQ so both increases and decreases are predictable. Tell us the expected range up front, not the single best guess.
