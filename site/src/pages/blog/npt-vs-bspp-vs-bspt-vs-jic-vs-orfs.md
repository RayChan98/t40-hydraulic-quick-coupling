## Intro — The Coupling Fits, the Thread Doesn't

You have specified the perfect ISO 7241 Series A coupling in 1/2". It arrives, you thread it onto the hose... and it won't go. Or it goes, leaks, and strips.

The ISO profile defines how the two coupling halves connect. It says nothing about the threads on the back of the coupling — the end that attaches to your hose or machine port. That is decided by the **thread standard**, and getting it wrong is the single most common reason a "correct" coupling fails on the first install.

There are six thread families you will meet on hydraulic quick couplings: NPT/NPTF, BSPP (G), BSPT (R), JIC (37° flare), ORFS, and metric (M). This guide explains what each is, where it dominates, how to tell them apart, and how to order so the threads match the first time.

## The Six Threads at a Glance

| Thread | Full name | Type | Seal method | Dominant markets |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| NPT / NPTF | National Pipe Thread (tapered) | Tapered pipe | Thread sealant / interference fit | US, Canada, Latin America |
| BSPP (G) | British Standard Pipe Parallel | Parallel pipe | Bonded washer or O-ring | Europe, Asia, Commonwealth |
| BSPT (R) | British Standard Pipe Tapered | Tapered pipe | Thread sealant | Commonwealth, Asia, Europe |
| JIC (37°) | Joint Industry Council 37° flare | Flare | Metal-to-metal 37° cone | US mobile equipment, agriculture |
| ORFS | O-Ring Face Seal | Straight thread + face O-ring | Face O-ring | High-pressure US mobile & industrial |
| Metric (M) | Metric straight thread (DIN 3852 etc.) | Straight | O-ring or washer | European machinery, global OEM |

## NPT and NPTF — The Tapered US Workhorse

NPT is a **tapered** pipe thread: the male thread gets wider toward the shoulder, so the flanks wedge together as you tighten. It seals on the thread interference (plus sealant). NPTF ("dryseal") is a refined version with controlled crests/roots, designed to seal without sealant.

- How to recognize: the thread visibly tapers; the male end is slightly pointed; a 1/2" NPT thread measures ~0.84" OD at the gage point.
- Gotcha: NPT male into BSPP female **looks** close (both ~14 threads/inch at 1/2") but the pitch angles differ — BSPP is 55°, NPT is 60°. They will start, then bind and leak.
- Where you see it: US-made hydraulic power units, US tractors, compressor fittings, anything with a pipe thread in North America.

## BSPP (G) — The Parallel European Standard

BSPP is a **parallel** (straight) thread: the male and female are cylindrical. It seals with a **bonded washer** or an O-ring at the face, not on the threads. That makes it reusable without re-taping, which is why European and Asian manufacturers love it.

- How to recognize: straight thread, 55° thread angle, flat sealing face with a washer groove; the size marking uses "G" (e.g. G1/2).
- Gotcha: BSPP male into BSPT female (or vice versa) partially engages but does not seal properly — BSPT relies on taper wedging.
- Where you see it: European hydraulic systems, most Chinese factory couplings, pumps, valves, cylinder ports, and the "G" ports on gear motors.

## BSPT (R) — The Tapered Commonwealth Cousin

BSPT is the **tapered** version of BSP — same 55° angle as BSPP but the male tapers and seals on thread interference with sealant. If a British or Commonwealth machine shows "R" or "Rp/Rc" thread markings, it is BSP family.

- How to recognize: tapered male, 55° angle, sealant required; often confused with NPT at a glance (measure the angle — 55° vs 60° — with a thread gauge).
- Gotcha: BSPT into NPT = guaranteed leak. This is the classic "fittings look identical" trap.

## JIC (37°) — The US Mobile Flare

JIC is not a pipe thread at all — it is a **flare** connection: the male has a 37° cone, the female has a matching 37° seat, and the union nut pulls the cone into the seat for a metal-to-metal seal.

- How to recognize: the distinctive 37° flared cone on the male, hex nut over the cone.
- Gotcha: JIC (37°) vs SAE 45° flare (automotive/refrigeration) look similar — do not mix them. JIC is 37°.
- Where you see it: US agricultural and construction hydraulics, hose assemblies, adapters.

## ORFS — The O-Ring Face Seal

ORFS (O-Ring Face Seal) uses a **straight thread** with an O-ring seated in a groove on the flat face. Tighten the nut, the O-ring compresses against the flat face — zero-leak, very high pressure rating, tolerant of vibration.

- How to recognize: straight thread + visible O-ring in the face groove; the female has the flat sealing face.
- Where you see it: high-pressure US mobile systems, pumps, motors, and any circuit that cannot tolerate leaks (e.g. fan drives, charge circuits).

## Metric (M) — The OEM World

Metric straight threads (DIN 3852 parts 1 & 2, ISO 6149, etc.) appear on European and Asian OEM components. They seal with an O-ring (port) or a bonded washer (external thread).

- How to recognize: metric pitch markings (e.g. M22×1.5); if a caliper reads a round millimeter value, it's metric.
- Gotcha: a 1/2" BSPP (G1/2, ~20.96mm OD) is very close to M22×1.5 (22mm OD) — one of the most common mis-threads in the field.

## The 30-Second Identification Flow

1. **Is it tapered?** Yes → check angle: 60° = NPT, 55° = BSPT. No → go to 2.
2. **Is there a flare cone or an O-ring face?** 37° cone = JIC. O-ring in face groove = ORFS. Flat face + washer groove = BSPP.
3. **Is it metric?** Measure OD in mm with a caliper and count threads — if the numbers are clean millimeters, it's metric.
4. **When in doubt**, take a photo of the male and female threads side by side and send it to the supplier. A photo resolves more order mistakes than any datasheet.

## FAQ

**Is BSPP the same as G thread?**
Yes — BSPP is the parallel British Standard Pipe thread, marked "G" (e.g. G1/2). "BSP" alone usually means BSPP in Asia, but always confirm.

**Can I use PTFE tape on BSPP?**
BSPP seals at the face, not the threads. Tape is unnecessary and can interfere with the washer seal — use a bonded washer or O-ring instead.

**NPT vs BSPT — which is more common?**
NPT dominates the Americas; BSPT is common in the Commonwealth and parts of Asia. On Chinese-made couplings you'll typically find BSPP (G) as the default, with NPT as the common export option.

**Why does my 1/2" coupling have a 1/2" NPT thread that measures 21mm?**
Because 1/2" NPT nominal size is not the actual OD (it's ~21.3mm / 0.84"). Nominal pipe sizes are historical. This confuses everyone once — now it won't confuse you.

**Which thread is best for high pressure?**
ORFS and JIC handle high pressure with vibration resistance; NPT relies on tapered interference and is more sensitive to over-tightening (cracking ports). For new designs, ORFS or BSPP-with-O-ring are the modern choices.
